## NARRATIVE

On 05/09/2026 at approximately 18:35 hours, I responded to 199 Willow Lane, Manchester, CT regarding a reported motor vehicle collision. Upon arrival I observed a white 2021 Subaru Forester parked in the driveway of the residence with damage to the rear bumper area, trunk lid, and driver-side rear quarter panel. A residential mailbox post at the end of the driveway was broken and lying on the ground with white paint transfer matching the Subaru.

The driver of the Forester, Maria Elena Rodriguez, stated she was unloading groceries from the vehicle when she heard a loud noise and found the rear of the vehicle damaged. She claimed a dark SUV struck her vehicle and fled the scene. She was unable to provide a license plate or identifying details.

I reviewed footage from the claimant's exterior home security camera, which recorded the vehicle reversing slowly from the driveway at 18:28 and contacting the mailbox post and a small utility pole at the head of the driveway. No other vehicle appears in the footage prior to or during the event.

**Statement of Maria Rodriguez**:
> "I was standing at the back of the car when I heard a bang. I thought someone hit me from behind. I didn't get a plate because I was in shock. I saw a dark SUV pull away, but I didn't get close enough to read the plate." 

**Statement of Olivia Chen** (neighbor at 201 Willow Lane):
> "I was on my porch and saw Maria backing out of her driveway. She kept reversing and then hit the mailbox and the small pole at the end of the driveway. I didn't see any other car there." 

The damage pattern on the Subaru and the broken mailbox are consistent with a single-vehicle backing impact, not a third-party hit-and-run. There was no visible paint transfer from another vehicle.
